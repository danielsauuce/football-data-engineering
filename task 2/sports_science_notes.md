# Note from the Sports Science Department

*Forwarded to the data team. Informal guidance, not an exhaustive specification.*

Hi all,

A few things we look at when we sanity-check GPS data after a session. This is from experience rather than a formal rulebook, so treat it as a starting point and come back to us when something is unclear.

- Nobody in our squad is getting anywhere near 37 km/h. Anything above roughly 36 km/h is almost always a GPS spike rather than a real sprint.
- Heart rate above about 220 bpm is not believable. A reading of 0 normally means the strap lost contact.
- Poor satellite lock (fewer than ~6 satellites, or a high HDOP) makes the position and speed data unreliable for that period.
- Total distance for a 75-minute training session above roughly 12 km would be very unusual for us. Whole-session totals should be in the same ballpark as the drill totals added together.
- Sometimes a player swaps a vest mid-session (battery or fit issue), so the same player can appear on two devices.
- Goalkeepers have very different numbers from outfield players. Please do not judge them against outfield ranges.
- Thresholds can change: pre-season, return-to-play and match-day-minus-one sessions all look different, and we revise our ranges each season.

Most importantly: we would rather be told "this looks odd, please check" than have data silently removed. We are the ones who decide whether something is genuinely wrong.
